Recommended Ideal Loadout — Mechanics Guide

This public guide explains how MFA builds, evaluates and ranks the Recommended Ideal Loadout. MFA is a deterministic community tool; its fracture equations and equipment data are community-calibrated and are not official CIG-published equations.

← Back to MFA

1. What “ideal” means in MFA

The Recommended Ideal Loadout is a target-driven planning result. It asks: given this rock and the selected mission constraints, which vessel combination and equipment configuration ranks highest under the selected objective?

Fleet Planner
What is physically present, fitted and active now.
Recommendation vessel pool
Which vessel types the solver is allowed to consider.
Available to assist
Post-recommendation confirmation that a required vessel can actually deploy.
Target Acquisition
Mass, resistance and instability used by the ideal solver.

2. Inputs and eligibility

The ideal solver reads rock mass, base resistance, base instability, optimizer objective, maximum ideal fleet size, Active-module permission, gadget permission and vessel-type eligibility.

The recommendation checkboxes are authoritative. If MOLE is unchecked, MOLE count is forced to zero. The same rule applies to Prospector and Golem.

Fleet Planner influence: none. Current Fleet Planner quantities, fitted loadouts and Active/Available/Standby state do not force a vessel type into the ideal recommendation.

3. Strategy classification

MFA selects the first matching deterministic strategy:

  1. Hazard / Instability — instability > 70
  2. Resistance Breaker — resistance > 60
  3. Stabilization — instability > 45
  4. Heavy Cluster — mass > 14,000 kg
  5. Standard — otherwise

Each strategy provides a baseline role/loadout template for MOLE, Prospector and Golem. MFA then generates Primary, Backup A and Backup B equipment variants.

4. Fleet-combination search

MFA enumerates every permitted vessel-count combination up to Max Fleet. With Prospector + Golem selected and Max Fleet = 3, the search includes Prospector-only, Golem-only and mixed Prospector/Golem plans.

1 Prospector
2 Prospectors
3 Prospectors
1 Golem
2 Golems
3 Golems
1 Prospector + 1 Golem
2 Prospectors + 1 Golem
1 Prospector + 2 Golems

For each composition, MFA walks the available equipment variants and, when gadgets are allowed, evaluates every gadget choice.

Current limitation: repeated vessels of the same type share the same selected variant. MFA does not yet fully optimize Prospector #1 as a Breaker and Prospector #2 as a different Stabilizer inside the same candidate.

5. Fracture mechanics used to evaluate each candidate

Every candidate goes through the same protected 4.10.1 runtime calculation used by MFA's deterministic Fracture Verdict.

Raw head power = base head power × module power multipliers

Resistance transfer factor = max(0, 1 - effective resistance / 100)

Effective head power = raw head power × resistance transfer factor

Total effective power = Σ effective head power

Max breakable mass = 5 × total effective power

Viable when:
Max breakable mass ≥ target rock mass

The displayed required raw power is:

Required power = rock mass / (5 × final transfer factor)

Instability modifiers from active heads are combined through their geometric mean, then the gadget instability modifier is applied.

6. Fracture margin

Margin % = (available raw power - required raw power)
           / required raw power × 100

A larger margin does not automatically win. The selected objective determines where margin sits in the ranking.

7. How objectives rank candidates

Minimum ships — default

  1. Viable before non-viable
  2. Fewer vessel hulls
  3. Larger fracture margin

Important: this means one viable MOLE normally beats two or three vessels under Minimum Ships.

Maximum fracture margin

  1. Viable before non-viable
  2. Larger fracture margin
  3. Fewer vessels

Minimum instability

  1. Viable before non-viable
  2. Fewer vessels
  3. Lower Final Instability
  4. Larger margin

Minimum consumables

  1. Viable before non-viable
  2. Fewer vessels
  3. Fewer Active modules + gadget uses
  4. Larger margin

8. Why 1 × MOLE appears frequently

Under Minimum Ships, the cost unit is vessel hull count. One MOLE is counted as one vessel; two Prospectors are counted as two vessels. The solver does not yet normalize this against mining-head count, crew count, ownership, travel time or operational burden.

1 vessel < 2 vessels

Therefore “Minimum ships” means exactly minimum vessel hull count, not minimum crew, minimum heads or minimum logistical effort.

9. Available to assist

After MFA selects the ideal plan, each required vessel receives an Available to assist confirmation. This only changes NOT CONFIRMED / CONFIRMED AVAILABLE and READY / NOT READY status. It does not recalculate the ideal plan.

If a recommended vessel cannot deploy, change the recommendation-vessel pool or mission constraints and recalculate.

10. Reproducing the result

  1. Create the exact recommended vessel count in Fleet Planner.
  2. Set the recommended vessels Active.
  3. Fit the exact mining heads shown.
  4. Fit every recommended module.
  5. Switch every recommended Active module ON.
  6. Select the recommended gadget.
  7. Do not leave extra vessels Active.

The Fracture Verdict then represents the reproduced real-fleet configuration.

11. Current non-modeled operational factors

The current ideal solver does not optimize player ownership, exact future vessel quantities, crew count, normalized mining-head cost, travel time, fuel/logistics, loss risk, player skill, coordination latency or independently specialized duplicate vessels.

Read the result as: the highest-ranked deterministic fracture plan under the constraints and objective you selected.

12. Public auditability

Readable development source remains available in the repository. The protected Alpha branch is used for development and validation; LIVE is promoted through Main. Production JavaScript may be obfuscated for casual source hardening, but MFA remains open source.
